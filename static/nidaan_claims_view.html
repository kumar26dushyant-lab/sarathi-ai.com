One Claims View

Claim Pipeline, Claims Dashboard and All Claims are three drawings of the same data. What a claim actually needs is one place that shows its whole chain — where it came from, who touched it, what was paid, and where it is now.

01

You are right, and here is the proof

I checked before answering. All three screens call the same endpoint.

ScreenCallsWhat it draws
Claims Dashboard/claims?limit=500 Counter tiles, then a list of active claims
Claim Pipeline/claims?limit=400 The same claims as columns by stage, filtered by origin
All Claims/claims?…filters The same claims as a table, a board or cards, plus archive and bulk actions
So the difference is not the data

It is only the shape. One counts, one columns, one tabulates — over an identical list. That is three places to keep in step, three places to fix a bug, and three places a person has to check before they believe they have seen everything.

02

What each actually contributes

Before merging anything, what would genuinely be lost:

FromWorth keepingBecomes
Claims DashboardThe counters — how many, how much, how many stuck A strip across the top of the one view
Claim PipelineSeeing the shape — where claims pile up The Board view, one of three
All ClaimsSearch, filters, archive, bulk actions, the dense table The Table view and the toolbar
Nothing is lost

Every capability survives. What disappears is the choice of which screen to open, which was never a useful decision for anybody to make.

The chain — the part you actually asked for

“Origin, chain, where it comes from” is the real request. Today that is scattered: origin on one screen, payment on another, Level-2 position on a third. It should be one strip on the claim, readable in a glance, in the order the claim actually happened.

Why the chain matters more than the counters

A counter tells you how many. The chain tells you how this one got here — which is the question asked when a branch partner rings, when a payment is queried, or when somebody wants to know why a case has taken four months. That answer currently requires opening three screens and remembering what was on the first.

05

What I would be careful about

  • Muscle memory. People have learned where things are, however inefficient. I would keep the old menu entries for a fortnight, pointing at the new view with a one-line note, rather than removing them overnight.
  • One screen doing everything gets slow. 124 claims is nothing; 5,000 is not. The table must page and the counters must be a separate, cached query, or the merge trades three fast screens for one slow one.
  • Permissions differ today. All Claims is admin-only; the Dashboard is open to everyone. Merging means deciding that once — my suggestion: everyone sees the view, and the admin-only actions (archive, bulk, re-attribute) stay admin-only.
The one real risk

Merging three screens into one is easy to start and easy to leave half-done, at which point there are four. If we do it, the old three come out in the same release the new one goes in — with the fortnight of redirects, and then they are gone.

06

Questions

Q11
Does L2 Claims merge too?

There is also an L2 Claims entry. Under the bucket system its job is largely taken over by Consolidation. My instinct: fold it into the one Claims view as a saved filter, and let the buckets own the working screens. Agree?

Q12
What belongs on the counter strip?

I have guessed: all claims, in Level-2, paid-not-started, winnable-unpaid, disputed value, recovered. Those are the numbers I would watch — but they are yours to choose.

Q13
Archived — a filter or a place?

Archived is its own menu entry today. Inside one view it could simply be a filter (Show: active · archived · all), which removes another entry from the sidebar.
